Pausing has the best-evidenced thresholds in the engine (De Jong and Bosker tested every cut-off from 20 ms to 1000 ms and found 250 to 300 optimal; placement-over-presence is the oldest replicated result in the field) and no speaker-level distribution anywhere, because the field reports distributions OF PAUSES rather than of speakers. Pace is four-ways convergent on 148 words a minute and its spread is two indirect estimates agreeing. Variety cannot use a published figure at all: type-token ratio falls as a sample grows, so a corpus number is a different measurement wearing the same name, which is the same class of error as the filler construct mismatch caught one trait earlier. **The one audit that ran before the session limit changed both of its trait's parameters and caught the proposal misreporting its own evidence**: it had claimed no published dispersion exists for English filled pauses, where Clark and Fox Tree section 7.3 gives 65 English speakers, 1.2 to 88.5 per 1,000 words, median 17.3. That error was not safe in either direction, since it licensed borrowing a spread from a Hungarian secondary source and the borrowed value was the FLOOR of what the English data supports. Centre corrected 4.53 → 3.0 per hundred words and treated as a ceiling; spread corrected 0.75 → 1.0. Three audits never ran and `docs/percentiles.md` says so in its method section rather than omitting it. **Three findings here are about constants the app already ships and none of them waits on a ring**: `fillerScore` pins the median speaker at 15 out of 100 and everyone above the 75th at zero, `repairScore` scores the average human 3 to 16, and the 130 to 160 pace zone is the 26th to the 65th percentile and charges an energetic 180 words a minute exactly what it charges a laboured 110, which the perception literature does not support on the fast side at all.
