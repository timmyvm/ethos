# Ethos — Voice Guide & Screen Copy

Everything below is derived from your own recorded transcript. The rules aren't invented; each one is a thing you already do when you talk.

---

## Part 1 — The seven rules

### 1. Talk to one person, and admit you know why they're here
Your line: *"Hey. I know why you're here."*

Don't open with a claim about the user. Open with an acknowledgement. "You already know the gap" tells them something about themselves. "I know why you're here" tells them something about you. The second one is warm, the first is a horoscope.

### 2. Say it, then stop
Your line: *"And then I'll say nothing more."*

This is the single most important rule and you found it yourself. The instinct to add one more clarifying sentence is the instinct that ruined the current copy. Write the line. Delete the sentence after it.

### 3. No abstraction you can't cash out
Your line: *"What does earn the room even mean?"*

If a phrase doesn't name an action or a number, it's decoration. Ban: earn the room, unlock your potential, find your voice, command presence, level up. Test — can the user do the thing the sentence describes? If not, cut it.

### 4. Numbers do the persuading
Your line: *"You can go to an articulation coach, which is five thousand dollars and heaps of commitment. Or sixty seconds a day."*

$5,000 vs 60 seconds. That's the entire pitch and it needed no adjectives. Reach for a real number before you reach for an emotive word.

### 5. Start sentences with And or But. Keep them short.
You do this constantly when talking. It's the main reason your speech has rhythm and the app copy doesn't. Fragments are fine. Sentences of three words are fine.

### 6. Repeat, don't escalate
Your line: *"People just respect you more, just subconsciously. People just subconsciously respect you more."*

When a human wants to emphasise something they say it twice. When an AI wants to emphasise something it finds a bigger word. Never trade up to "profoundly" or "fundamentally."

### 7. Undersell the outcome
You never promised transformation in the transcript. You said people will subconsciously respect them more. That's a small, specific, believable claim, and it's far more persuasive than anything grand. Keep the promises this size.

## Part 2 — The screen template

Every explanation screen in the app follows this shape. Nothing else goes on the page.

```
TITLE                    (2–4 words)
One line of what it is.  (one sentence, no second sentence)

How to do this           (bold)
· tactic
· tactic
· tactic

[ACTION BUTTON]

fine print               (bottom, small, muted)
```

If something doesn't fit this template, it isn't copy — it's a tooltip, a collapsed "why this works," or it gets deleted.

## Part 4 — Two things to reconsider

**"Earn the room."** You questioned it out loud and your instinct was right. It's a mood, not a meaning. "Practice being worth listening to" survives better because "practice" is a verb the user can picture doing. If you want a third option pulled from your own transcript: *Sixty seconds a day.* It's not poetic, but nobody has ever had to ask what it means.

**The compound interest line.** You reached for 1.01^365 and fumbled the number in the recording (it's about 37x, not 1.36). Worth knowing, but I'd leave it out of the copy anyway. Every habit app on the store uses that graph, so it reads as a genre convention rather than as you. Your $5,000-vs-60-seconds comparison is a stronger version of the same argument and it's yours.

## Part 5 — How to write the rest yourself

Do exactly what you just did. Open a recording, explain the next lesson out loud to an imaginary mate, transcribe it, then cut everything except the parts that sound like you. You produced better copy in a rambling voice note than the app currently has on any screen, and it took you about four minutes.

Then run it against rule 2. Almost every fix is a deletion.
